Wrap edges turns the grid into a torus. Seed a single cell on a 64 or 108 world with the classic rule and it grows into itself, then freezes into a wallpaper that never changes again. Larger worlds cycle instead of freezing — 300 settles into a 12‑state loop.

The rule. A cell holds a level, not a bit. The neighbourhood factor is the sum of the eight neighbours' levels, and it selects a step up or down the ladder: too little support and the cell starves, just enough and it grows, too much and overcrowding pushes it back down.

Why dots. A solid line dies instantly — touching cells overcrowd each other on the first generation. Spaced far enough apart, each cell is alone long enough to bloom, and the blooms meet as fronts.
